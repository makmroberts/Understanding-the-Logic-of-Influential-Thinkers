# Graph indices

Generated by `scripts/graph_indices.py`. Indices follow Ozesmi &
Ozesmi (2004); the signed-graph form follows Axelrod (1976).

**The sign is argumentative, not causal.** It means *backs* / *pushes
back on*, not *increases* / *decreases*. See the script docstring.

## 2. Map structure, against the FCM literature

| | this corpus | Ozesmi & Ozesmi (2004) |
|---|---|---|
| variables per map | 17.9 ± 4.0 | 23 ± 2 |
| connections per map | 22.8 ± 7.5 | 37 ± 3 |

Comparable in size, roughly half as densely connected. That is the
expected signature of an argument map rather than a causal web: it is
built to converge on a claim, not to close loops - an elicited FCM
closes feedback loops, and these maps deliberately do not.

| scope | variables | connections | density | hierarchy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| whole corpus | 359 | 455 | 0.0035 | 0.0001 |
| mean per article | 17.9 | 22.8 | 0.0765 | 0.0372 |
| variable-level map | 34 | 541 | 0.4822 | 1.415 |

Corpus-wide density is near zero by construction - the 20 maps are 20 disconnected components, joined only by the concept layer (9 of 358 nodes). The per-article figures are the meaningful ones.

**Reading the hierarchy index.** MacDonald's h normalises by N^3 while
the outdegree variance it sums grows only with N, so it approaches 1
only for a star - one node with outdegree near N-1. It measures
star-ness, not layeredness. These maps are layered L0-L6 with a peak
outdegree around 4 out of a possible 16, so a near-zero h is correct
and is NOT evidence the maps are flat. The variable-level map scores
1.415 because a handful of variables really do
dominate the outflow there.

Variable character, per article (mean):

- ordinary: 11.6 per map (232 total)
- receiver: 3.7 per map (74 total)
- transmitter: 2.4 per map (48 total)
- isolated: 0.2 per map (5 total)

The 5 isolates are all `question` nodes - the known mermaid artifact (question-to-priors links are drawn with the layout-only `~~~`, which carries no meaning, so no edge was recorded). Not a data problem, but it does mean every map's question node is unreachable by graph traversal.

## 3. Which subjects are central to his reasoning

`transmitter_bias` is (out - in) / (out + in) over the claims carrying
a term: **positive means he argues FROM it** (it behaves like a prior or a mechanism), **negative means he argues TO it** (it behaves like a conclusion). This is the ranking signal the retrieval path lacks.

**Caveat on the measure.** Bias washes out for ubiquitous terms. `us`, `china`, `tariffs` and `import-volumes` all sit on both ends of most edges, so they land near 0 and the number says nothing. Bias is informative for mid- and low-frequency terms, and centrality is the number to read for the common ones.

#### The one finding worth stating outright

`customs` runs 48 out / 43 in (bias +0.05), while `balance-of-payments` runs 34 out / 40 in (bias -0.08).

He argues **from** customs data and **about** balance-of-payments
data. Customs is a premise he does not defend; BoP is a contested
object he attacks and others defend. That is his measurement-critique
stance - and here it falls out of graph structure rather than being
asserted. It is also the strongest evidence that `data_basis`
earned its place as a facet: no other facet separates these claims.

Compare `academic-econometrics` (+0.88), `national-accounts`
(+0.75) and `press-reporting` (+1.0): near-pure transmitters, cited
as inputs and never argued over. And `trade-agreements` at -1.0 on
the instrument facet - 0 out, 3 in - trade deals are only ever
something he argues toward, never a premise he reasons from.

## What is still missing for a causal map

Everything above projects ARGUMENT structure. A true Axelrod map needs
the direction of effect between variables - "weak domestic demand
raises the surplus" - and that sign sits in the mechanism prose,
unannotated. Adding a `causal_sign` to mechanism nodes is the next
annotation pass, and it is what would let the graph answer "if saving
rises, what does he think happens to the surplus?"
