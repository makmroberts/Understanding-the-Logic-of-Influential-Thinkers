# Passage linking

The last link in the provenance chain: which paragraph of the article carries each
claim. Generated by `scripts/link_passages.py`.

Links are graded, and the grade is part of the data. **`anchored` means the claim and
the paragraph share a distinctive literal** - a figure or a rare proper noun - so a
human can confirm it at a glance. **`candidate` is semantic overlap only: a reviewing
aid, not a citation.** Never render a candidate as a source.

- 373 claims over 21 articles, 715 paragraphs
- **confirmed by hand: 0 (0%)**
- **anchored: 72 (19%)**
- candidate: 281 (75%)
- none: 20 (5%)

## The pattern by role, which is also a validity check

Every claim comes from somewhere in the article; what differs is HOW. Evidence
**states** a figure. A mechanism **abstracts** a passage. A hidden assumption
**presupposes** one - the paragraph only works if the claim is true - which is why
a hidden assumption that *states* a literal is the anomaly, not one that fails to.
Deviations from the expected column are worth an annotator's time.

`abstracts` from `presupposes`; `scripts/review_passages.py` is where a human

## Flagged by that check

- **3 `hidden_assumption` claims reach no paragraph at all.** Worth asking where the annotator got them: a warrant abstracted from nothing in the article may be a prior carried in from his wider work, which is a legitimate finding, or the matcher may simply have missed it.

## How to read a row

```
python scripts/link_passages.py --show setser_aug_14_24#E1
```

Paragraph numbers are only meaningful against the extraction they came from, so
every row carries that page's `page_sha256`. If CFR re-flows an article the hash
changes and the citation is invalidated rather than silently wrong.

The article text lives in `internal/extracted/`, which is gitignored - so these
rows are pointers into local files, by design.
